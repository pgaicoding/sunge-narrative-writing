# Fact Boundaries for Real-Person Narratives

Read this before writing narrative nonfiction, profiles, case studies, memoir, current-event essays, or any piece involving identifiable people.

## Choose a Truth Contract

| Mode | What may be asserted | Required handling |
|---|---|---|
| fiction | invented events and composite characters | avoid recognizable private allegations about real people |
| narrative nonfiction | verified events and clearly attributed recollection | maintain a claim ledger and preserve uncertainty |
| brand/case story | authorized facts, records, and approved experience | distinguish observation, result, and interpretation |
| hybrid | documented facts plus invented connective material | label the hybrid and identify reconstruction |

Do not silently switch modes because a source is vivid.

## Source Status Is Not Event Status

Classify each material item:

- `observed`: directly witnessed by the writer;
- `recorded`: supported by a primary record, log, document, or media file;
- `attributed`: a named source says it happened;
- `disputed`: relevant parties or records conflict;
- `inferred`: a conclusion drawn from other evidence;
- `invented`: created for fiction.

For causal links and hidden questions, also record the causal status: `verified`, `participant explanation`, `editorial inference`, or `unverified`. A narratively useful hypothesis does not become a fact by appearing repeatedly.

A public first-person post is evidence that the author published those words. It is not, by itself, proof of the private events described. A disclaimer such as “fiction” makes literal factual reuse less defensible, not more.

## Claim Ledger

For every publication-sensitive claim, record:

| Claim | Status | Source | Safe wording | Keep? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Match wording to evidence:

- `X wrote/said/alleged...` for attributed claims;
- `According to the dated record...` for documented facts;
- `I remember...` for personal memory;
- `I inferred...` for interpretation;
- `The available material does not establish...` when certainty is absent.

## Hard Stops

Do not invent or present as fact:

- private sexual, reproductive, medical, financial, or family conduct;
- motives, diagnoses, consent, or internal states;
- quotations not captured in a reliable source;
- precise numbers, dates, places, or transactions used only to make a scene feel real;
- composite behavior attached to an identifiable person.

For public-facing work with legal, reputational, privacy, or safety exposure, narrow the piece to the public text, public response, or writing mechanism. Seek appropriate editorial or legal review when the intended publication still depends on disputed real-person allegations.

## Transformation Check

When learning from a source text:

1. write the mechanism in abstract terms;
2. close the source;
3. choose a different subject, scene order, motif, scale, and sentence rhythm;
4. compare for distinctive phrasing, plot sequence, object combination, and character mapping;
5. revise until the new work stands without recognition of the source.

If the source cannot be accessed for comparison, say only that the new work was redesigned at the mechanism level and that similarity remains unverified. Do not claim a completed source-distance audit.

Credit inspiration when appropriate, but do not treat attribution as permission to copy protected expression.
